# Positional standards (standards-v1)

Generated by `php bin/console app:standards:derive` from the 2024/25 and 2025/26 league seasons of the
Top-5 dataset; do not edit by hand. The frozen numbers are in `config/standards/standards-v1.json`; the model
reads them from there. The explanation for readers is in [methodology.md](methodology.md).

## The question

For each role (striker, winger, attacking, central and defensive midfielder, full-back, centre-back,
goalkeeper): which statistics show that a player is good at his role, and how much should each count?
The answer is measured, not chosen. Football is won by goal difference, so a statistic counts for a role
when the players who do more of it play in teams that win, in both seasons, and when it is a stable trait
of the player rather than luck.

## Method

Players with 900+ league minutes in a season, compared within their role. Per role and statistic:

1. **Measure**: per 90 minutes (counts) or as a success rate (successes / attempts). Failures (errors,
   lost duels, cards) count against the player.
2. **Small samples**: a value is pulled toward the role's average in proportion to how little it rests on.
   How strongly follows from the data: if a statistic over a typical sample of n̄ matches (or attempts)
   repeats between seasons with correlation ρ, a player's value keeps n / (n + k) of its distance from
   the average, with k = n̄ (1 − ρ) / ρ.
3. **Scale**: counts on a square-root scale (the standard variance-stabilising transform for counts), so
   a few extreme values do not dominate.
4. **Possession**: a team with the ball passes more; one without defends more. The part of the statistic
   that the team's possession explains (a linear fit within the role) is removed, and so is the part of
   the team's goal difference that its possession explains. Possession is estimated from passing.
5. **Link with winning**: the correlation between the statistic and the team's goal difference per match.
   A statistic is **used** only when that link is positive and significant (one-sided 5%, z = 1.645 / √n)
   in **each season on its own**: a link that does not replicate is likely chance.
6. **Repeatability**: the correlation of the statistic for the same players in the same role in both seasons.
7. **Counted once**: used statistics that correlate 0.8 or more (average linkage) form one **measure**: the
   average of their standardised values.
8. **Weight** of a measure = its link with winning × its repeatability, scaled so a role's weights add up to
   100%. A measure that goes with winning but barely repeats is mostly luck, and counts little.

Candidates are all statistics in the data whose direction is clear from their definition; the others are listed
at the end with the reason. Coverage: a statistic must be known for 80% of a role's players.

The only fixed numbers above are statistical conventions (significance level, "very strong" correlation,
minimum minutes), the same for every role and statistic.

## Validation

Standards derived from 2024/25 alone (its team results decide; repeatability uses 2025/26's
statistics, never its results) rate the 2024/25 players. Two tests on 2025/26:

- **Team results**: a team's 2025/26 goal difference against the average 2024/25 rating of the players it
  fields in 2025/26 (minutes-weighted; teams whose rated players played 20%+ of its minutes); also beyond its
  2024/25 goal difference.
- **Repeatability**: how alike the same players are rated in both seasons.

| Method | Team results r | Beyond last season's results | Teams | Repeatability r | Players |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| The standards: weight = link with winning × repeatability | 0.37 | 0.00 | 82 | 0.59 | 1017 |
| The same measures, weighted equally | 0.34 | -0.03 | 82 | 0.50 | 1017 |
| Every candidate statistic, weighted equally ("every good statistic counts") | 0.39 | 0.12 | 82 | 0.52 | 1027 |
| The same measures, weighted by link with winning only | 0.34 | -0.07 | 82 | 0.49 | 1017 |

With about 80 teams a difference in r below 0.2 is within chance; the repeatability comparison, over
1,000+ players, is the sharper test.

## Statistics in the data that are not candidates

| Statistic | Why |
|---|---|
| Goals (all) | Non-penalty goals are the candidate: a penalty is a set chance, valued by penalty xG |
| Goals inside / outside the box, headed, left / right foot, free kicks, penalty goals | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once |
| Goals + assists | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once |
| Expected goals (incl. penalties) | Non-penalty xG is the candidate |
| Expected goals and expected assists (SofaScore) | A second model of the same thing: Understat's xG and xA are the candidates; SofaScore's only fill gaps in the contribution |
| xGChain | The sum of xGBuildup and his own shots' xG and key passes' xA, which are candidates: counted once |
| Shots, shots off target, shots inside / outside the box, shots from set pieces | A volume of attempts: its successes and its success rate are candidates instead |
| Passes, long balls, chipped passes, crosses, dribble attempts, own-half / opposition-half passes attempted | A volume of attempts: its successes and its success rate are candidates instead |
| Tackles | A volume of attempts: its successes and its success rate are candidates instead (tackles won) |
| Total duels won, total duels won % | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once (ground and aerial duels won, and their success rates) |
| Duels lost | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once (aerial duels lost; ground duels lost are in the ground-duel success rate) |
| Accurate passes, pass accuracy % | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once (accurate passes in own half and in the opposition half, and their accuracy) |
| Possession lost | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once (dispossessed, inaccurate passes, and failed dribbles in the dribble success rate) |
| Goal conversion % (SofaScore) | Includes penalties: goals per non-penalty shot is the candidate |
| Non-penalty goals minus xG | The difference of two candidates (non-penalty goals, npxG) |
| Hit the woodwork, own shots blocked | Outcomes of his own shots without a direction (neither a goal nor a miss by his doing); shots on target % and goals per shot cover his shooting |
| Direct red cards | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once (red cards) |
| Understat's shots, key passes and non-penalty goals | A second provider of the same counts (FBref and SofaScore) |
| Penalties taken, on target, hit the post, missed; penalty saves % | Rare, and a set chance, not open play |
| Goals conceded and clean sheets of outfield players | A team outcome while he is on the pitch, not his own doing (in the contribution: team defence) |
| Goals conceded, clean sheets (goalkeepers) | A team outcome while he is on the pitch, not his own doing (in the contribution: team defence); goals prevented measures the goalkeeper |
| Saves, saves inside / outside the box, caught, parried | Depend on how many shots his defence allows: goals prevented measures the goalkeeper |
| Save % | Depends on how hard the shots his defence allows are; goals prevented corrects for that |
| Penalties faced, goal kicks | No direction: how often, not how well |
| Runs out | A volume of attempts: its successes and its success rate are candidates instead (successful runs out) |
| SofaScore rating | Another provider's rating: the standards are built from the statistics themselves |
| Top speed | A single peak, not a count or a rate |
| Pass to assist, total attempt assist | Part or sum of another statistic, which is a candidate: counted once (key passes, assists) |
| Appearances, starts, team of the week | Playing time is in the contribution (season totals and replacement level) |
